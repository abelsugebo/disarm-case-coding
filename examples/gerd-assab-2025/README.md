# Worked example: GERD pride and "Assab / sea access" narratives (Ethiopia, 2025)

**Window:** 19 August to 24 October 2025
**Platforms:** X, YouTube
**Languages:** English, Amharic, Afaan Oromo, mixed
**Source:** *DISARM Case Study: AI-Generated Propaganda Linking GERD Pride to "Assab/Sea Access" Narratives*, Abel W. Sugebo, Inform Africa / HaqCheck, 2025 ([PDF](https://informafrica.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Disarm-Case-Study_-AI-Generated-Propaganda-Linking-GERD-Pride-To-_Assab_Sea-Access_-Narratives_final_inform_africa.pdf)). The work was carried out in the context of the Africa Digital Rights Fund with support from CIPESA.

## What happened

Around the 9 September 2025 inauguration of the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, online content framed national development pride as justification for future Red Sea access via Eritrea's Assab port. Three patterns stand out:

- **Synthetic and montage visuals** made the claim feel aspirational and inevitable (E06, E02, E01).
- **Clip migration:** a segment of a long-form YouTube interview was cut into a short clip on X, then quote-shared with sharper, more mobilizing hashtags (E11 → E10 → E09).
- **Multilingual amplification:** a widely viewed clip with Afaan Oromo audio framed sea access as a security issue (E08).

## How to read the files

- `evidence_log.csv` lists the 14 evidence items as the public report describes them. E03 and E07 are not described in the report and are marked as such. Fields the report does not state are left blank rather than inferred.
- `disarm_mapping.csv` reproduces Table 1 of the report. DISARM tactic IDs and phases were added for this repository from the DISARM Red Framework; the behaviors, evidence IDs, and confidence levels are unchanged from the report.

## Limits

The case documents observable behavior only and does not attribute intent or coordination to any actor. Engagement metrics were point-in-time. Some content may since have been edited or deleted.
